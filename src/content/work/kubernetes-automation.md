## The challenge

Kubernetes upgrades and recurring platform maintenance required manual work. The opportunity was to make those operations more repeatable while reducing the effort involved in keeping Azure Kubernetes Service environments up to date. Upgrade automation was part of a wider platform engineering effort spanning infrastructure, operational controls, and software delivery.

## My contribution

As a Senior Software Developer in DevOps at OpenText, I automated Kubernetes operations using Terraform and Terragrunt. The work included AKS upgrades, operational feature gates, and KMS key rotation. It brought recurring tasks into infrastructure-as-code workflows and reduced the amount of manual intervention needed for upgrades.

I also partnered with engineering teams to standardize infrastructure, deployment, testing, and release workflows. That collaboration connected the automation to the ways teams already worked and helped reduce friction across software delivery processes.

## The result

Manual Kubernetes upgrade effort fell to approximately one-quarter of the previous effort: a roughly 4× reduction. This measures the work required to perform an upgrade, rather than the speed of the cluster upgrade itself.

The broader focus was practical platform improvement: repeatable operations, consistent infrastructure workflows, and a better developer experience.
